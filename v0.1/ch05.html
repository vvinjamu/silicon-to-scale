Thermal, Power & TCO

The Economics of AI Infrastructure — Why a 700-Watt Chip Changes Everything About Building a Datacenter
Book: AI/ML Infrastructure from Silicon to Scale — V2 Edition  |  Author: Venkat Vinjam  |  Cross-ref: Ch 4: Memory Hierarchy Ch 7: GPU Kernels Ch 12: Benchmarking Ch 17: Telemetry
"Every watt you waste cooling hardware is a watt you didn't spend training a model. The most important equation in AI infrastructure isn't a matrix multiply — it's Power × PUE × $/kWh × 8,760 hours." — Principal Infrastructure Architect's Cost Axiom

The H100 SXM5 has a Thermal Design Power (TDP) of 700W. A rack of 8 DGX H100 nodes draws 120+ kilowatts. A 10,000-GPU training cluster consumes power comparable to a small town. At this scale, thermal management is not a facilities concern — it is a performance engineering concern. Thermal throttling directly reduces MFU. Poor power allocation inflates TCO by millions of dollars per year.

5.1 Where Heat Comes From — Semiconductor Power Dissipation

5.1.1 The Three Sources of Power Dissipation

POWER DISSIPATION IN CMOS CIRCUITS: P_total = P_dynamic + P_static + P_short_circuit 1. DYNAMIC POWER (60-75% of total): P_dynamic = alpha x C x V^2 x f Key: Power scales as V^2 -- reducing V by 10% cuts dynamic power by ~19%. This is the lever behind DVFS. 2. STATIC POWER / LEAKAGE (20-35%): P_static = V_dd x I_leakage At 4nm, leakage ~25% of total power even at zero workload. Idle GPUs draw 100-200W from leakage alone. 3. SHORT-CIRCUIT POWER (<5%): Brief PMOS+NMOS conduction during transitions. Not relevant to system engineers.

5.1.2 H100 Power Distribution

H100 SXM5 Power Distribution (700W TDP, estimated): COMPONENT POWER % of TDP ---------------------------------------------------- Tensor Cores (132 SMs x 4 TC) ~280W 40% HBM3 Interface + Controllers ~90W 13% NVLink 4.0 PHY (18 links) ~70W 10% CUDA Cores + Special Function ~70W 10% L2 Cache + Memory Fabric ~63W 9% PCIe Gen5 Interface ~35W 5% NVSwitch + I/O Fabric ~35W 5% Misc (clock gen, power mgmt) ~57W 8% ---------------------------------------------------- TOTAL 700W 100% Hotspot: TC clusters nearest HBM stacks run 15-25 deg C above average die temp.

5.1.3 Thermal Throttling Hierarchy

Every GPU has a maximum junction temperature (T_j_max). For H100 SXM: 83°C operational, 95°C absolute maximum. Throttling is not binary — it cascades:

⚠ The MFU Cost of Thermal Throttling A 10,000-GPU cluster where 15% of GPUs soft-throttle at 25% clock reduction loses 387 GPU-equivalents — worth $8.5M/year at $2.50/GPU-hour. Entirely recoverable with proper cooling. Always check DCGM throttle counters before attributing MFU loss to software.

5.2 Cooling Technologies for AI Infrastructure

5.2.1 Thermal Resistance Chain

T_junction = T_ambient + (P_gpu x R_theta_total) Air cooling R_theta ~0.07 C/W: DeltaT = 700 x 0.07 = 49 C Required ambient <= 83 - 49 = 34 C -- dangerously tight DLC R_theta ~0.03 C/W: DeltaT = 700 x 0.03 = 21 C Coolant inlet up to 62 C -- comfortable margin

5.2.2 Direct Liquid Cooling (DLC)

DLC brings coolant to GPU cold plates directly. With coolant inlet at 45°C: T_j = 45 + (700 × 0.02) = 59°C — 24°C below soft-throttle. Supports TDP up to 1,500W+, enables 100–300 kW/rack density, allows waste heat reuse at 55–65°C outlet. CapEx premium: $50,000–$200,000+ per CDU.

5.3 GPU Power Management — DVFS and Power States

5.3.1 DVFS — The 1ms Feedback Loop

DVFS FEEDBACK LOOP (every ~1ms on H100): 1. Power sensor reads current draw 2. Thermal sensor reads junction temp 3. If current_power > limit OR T_j > threshold: Reduce V_core by ~5mV, f_core by ~13MHz (--3% power per step) 4. If headroom exists: increase f_core (boost) Measured sustained SM clocks: Optimal DLC (25C coolant): 1,980 MHz -- baseline Good DLC (35C coolant): 1,830 MHz -- -7.6% MFU Air (well-ventilated): 1,650 MHz -- -16.7% MFU Air (hot aisle, 50C inlet): 1,470 MHz -- -25.8% MFU Hard throttle (83C T_j): ~1,000 MHz -- -49.5% MFU
💡 Sustained Clock ≠ Advertised Boost Clock H100 boost clock of 1,980 MHz is transient only. Under sustained 700W load, SM clocks stabilize at 1,600–1,800 MHz depending on cooling — a 9–24% gap never captured in burst benchmarks. Design capacity plans from DCGM sustained measurements, not spec sheets.

5.3.2 P-States

P-State Hierarchy (H100): P0 Maximum Performance: 700W | Full TDP, max clocks P1 High Performance: 500W | ~80% max clocks P2 Balanced: 350W | Moderate clocks P8 Low Power Idle: 125W | Initialized, not computing P12 Deep Idle: 40W | Maximum clock gating P0 <-> P8 transition: 10-50ms latency Inference servers: lock at P0 to avoid TTFT spikes Fleet cost: 100-150W/GPU idle P0 vs P8 -- material at scale

5.3.3 Power Capping

H100 Performance-per-Watt (GEMM, approximate): 500W --> 800 TFLOPS = 1.60 TFLOPS/W (+13.5% efficiency vs TDP) 600W --> 910 TFLOPS = 1.52 TFLOPS/W (+7.8% vs TDP) 700W --> 989 TFLOPS = 1.41 TFLOPS/W (baseline, worst efficiency) Last 100W of TDP buys only ~9% more performance at 14.3% more power. For $/token optimization: 500W may be more profitable than 700W TDP.

5.4 Datacenter Power Infrastructure

5.4.1 Power Delivery Chain

Utility (480V AC) --> UPS (96%) --> PDU (98%) --> PSU (94%) --> VRM (90%) --> GPU Cumulative: 0.985 x 0.96 x 0.98 x 0.94 x 0.90 = 80.7% efficient For every 1,000W at the GPU die: 1,239W from the utility grid. 239W of losses = heat that must also be cooled.

5.4.2 Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)

PUE = Total Facility Power / IT Equipment Power Example (10,000 H100 cluster): IT Power: 7,663 kW (GPUs + CPU + network + storage) Overhead: 1,027 kW (DLC + UPS losses + misc) PUE = 8,690 / 7,663 = 1.134 Benchmarks: 1.01-1.05 World-class (Google Oregon with waste heat reuse) 1.05-1.15 Excellent (modern hyperscaler with DLC) 1.20-1.30 Good (modern air-cooled) 1.40-1.60 Average enterprise datacenter >2.00 Legacy (half of IT power wasted) Cost impact: every 0.10 PUE improvement saves ~$3.1M/year (10K GPU, $0.05/kWh)

5.5 TCO Modeling for AI Infrastructure

5.5.1 TCO Formula and 3-Year Breakdown

TCO = CapEx + (Power_kW x PUE x $/kWh x 8,760 x years) + OpEx_other 3-YEAR TCO -- 10,000 GPU H100 Cluster: CapEx (3yr amortized): GPU hardware (~$30K avg): $3.0B -- dominates at 83% of TCO InfiniBand network: $64M Storage: $20M DLC + DC buildout + power: $95M Total CapEx: ~$3.18B (amortized: ~$1.06B/year) Annual OpEx: Power (7,663kW x 1.13 x 8,760 x $0.05): $34.4M HW maintenance (3% CapEx): $90.0M Staff (50 FTEs x $180K): $9.0M Software licenses + other: $13.0M Total Annual OpEx: ~$146M 3-Year Total: $3.18B + ($146M x 3) = ~$3.62B Cost/GPU-hour (80% util, 3yr): GPU-hours = 10,000 x 8,760 x 3 x 0.80 = 210M $/GPU-hr = $3,620M / 210M = ~$17.24 (owned H100) Compare: Cloud H100 on-demand = $3.50-4.50/GPU-hr Owned infrastructure only beats cloud at >65% sustained utilization

Worked Example 5.1

DLC vs. Air Cooling — True ROI Beyond Electricity Savings

Problem: Air (PUE 1.35) vs. DLC (PUE 1.10). DLC premium: $25M. At $0.05/kWh, when does DLC pay back — and what changes when MFU recovery is included?

Step 1: Annual electricity -- Air (PUE 1.35) Facility power = 7,663 kW x 1.35 = 10,345 kW Annual cost = 10,345 x 8,760 x $0.05 = $4.53M/year Step 2: Annual electricity -- DLC (PUE 1.10) Facility power = 7,663 kW x 1.10 = 8,429 kW Annual cost = 8,429 x 8,760 x $0.05 = $3.69M/year Step 3: Electricity savings = $4.53M - $3.69M = $0.84M/year Payback from electricity alone: $25M / $0.84M = 29.8 years ← Not viable! Step 4: Include MFU recovery (conservative: 15% GPUs throttle at 10% loss) Lost capacity = 1,500 GPUs x 10% = 150 GPU-equivalents At $2.50/GPU-hr: 150 x 8,760 x $2.50 = $3.29M/year recovered (Aggressive: 15% at 25.8% loss = $8.5M/year recovered) Step 5: Combined payback Conservative: $25M / ($0.84M + $3.29M) = 6.1 years Moderate: $25M / ($0.84M + $8.50M) = 2.7 years GOOD ROI Principal Lesson: DLC ROI is driven by performance recovery (MFU), NOT electricity savings alone. Pitch DLC on recovered GPU-hours, not on the power bill. The electricity saving is a rounding error.

5.6 Thermal Monitoring — Profiling Cookbook

🔧 PROFILING COOKBOOK — Thermal & Power Diagnostics
WHAT TO MEASURE FIRST:
  DCGM field 203 (GPU_TEMP)        alert: >78 C
  DCGM field 204 (MEM_TEMP)        alert: >90 C
  DCGM field 155 (SM_CLOCK)        detects throttling in real time
  DCGM field 240 (THROTTLE_REASON) non-zero = active throttle event
  DCGM field 112 (POWER_DRAW)      compare to configured power limit

COMMANDS:
  # Quick check -- all GPUs
  nvidia-smi --query-gpu=index,temperature.gpu,power.draw,clocks.sm,
    clocks_throttle_reasons.hw_slowdown,clocks_throttle_reasons.sw_thermal_slowdown
    --format=csv,noheader

  # Find throttling GPUs immediately
  nvidia-smi --query-gpu=index,temperature.gpu,
    clocks_throttle_reasons.hw_slowdown,clocks_throttle_reasons.sw_thermal_slowdown,
    power.draw --format=csv,noheader |
    awk -F',' '$3=="Yes" || $4=="Yes" {print "THROTTLE:",$0}'

  # DCGM real-time monitor (production clusters)
  dcgmi dmon -e 203,204,155,156,112,240

  # Prometheus/Grafana exporter
  dcgm-exporter &
  # Exposes: DCGM_FI_DEV_GPU_TEMP, DCGM_FI_DEV_SM_CLOCK,
  #          DCGM_FI_DEV_POWER_USAGE, DCGM_FI_DEV_THROTTLE_REASON

  # Set power cap
  nvidia-smi --power-limit=600        # All GPUs
  nvidia-smi -i 3 --power-limit=550   # Single GPU index 3

DECISION TREE when GPU_TEMP > 78 C:
  Check inlet temp (IPMI):
    Inlet >35 C?  YES: Facility issue (CRAC/CDU failure, hot aisle containment)
                  NO:  Server issue (fan RPM, DLC flow rate, TIM degradation)
  Can resolve in <30 min?
    YES: Monitor at 5-min intervals
    NO:  IMMEDIATE: power-cap to 500W, drain workloads gracefully
         Training: trigger async checkpoint NOW
         Inference: remove from load balancer, reduce batch size

MFU drops 15-30% with no code change?
  Check SM_CLOCK via DCGM -- below expected?
  YES: check THROTTLE_REASONS (thermal or power limit)
  Check NVSwitch temp: >90 C causes fabric BW drop 30-50%
  Check NVLink error counters + board BMC temperature

5.7 Thermal Considerations in System Design

5.7.1 Thermal-Aware Job Scheduling

THERMAL-AWARE SCHEDULING (SLURM + DCGM): thermal_score(node) = 1.0 - (T_j - T_nominal) / (T_max - T_nominal) placement_score = 0.7 x utilization_score + 0.3 x thermal_score Avoid placement if T_j > 76 C Result: throttling events reduced ~40-60% across cluster (Meta MWFS, Google Borg, NVIDIA Base Command Manager)

5.7.2 Batch Size and Thermal Load

Decode phase thermal profile: Batch=1: ~400-450W | T_j ~55-60 C | no throttling Batch=32: ~550-600W | T_j ~68-72 C | mild risk Batch=128: ~650-700W | T_j ~78-83 C | guaranteed throttle in air Prefill is always compute-bound regardless of sequence length. Power draws near TDP within 30-60 seconds of sustained prefill. Thermal governor pattern: if T_j > 76 C: reduce max_batch_size by 20% Prevents throttle-thrash cycles, stabilizes production MFU.

Key Takeaways — Chapter 5

Core Concepts
  • GPU power follows P = αCV²f; DVFS controls V and f together — reducing V by 10% cuts dynamic power by ~19% (quadratic), not 10% (linear). This is the physics behind every power optimization.
  • Thermal resistance governs sustainable performance: T_j = T_ambient + (P × R_theta). Air cooling at 700W leaves only a 1°C margin in a standard hot aisle — any ambient temperature spike triggers throttling.
  • PUE = Total Facility Power / IT Power. Every 0.10 PUE improvement on a 10,000-GPU cluster saves ~$3.1M/year. DLC enables PUE 1.03–1.15 vs. air cooling's 1.20–1.60.
  • H100 advertised boost clock (1,980 MHz) is transient. Sustained frequency under 700W load is 1,600–1,800 MHz — a 9–19% gap that burst benchmarks never capture. Always plan from sustained DCGM measurements.
Cost & Productivity Impact
  • 15% of GPUs throttling at 25% clock reduction on a 10,000-GPU cluster wastes 387 GPU-equivalents — $8.5M/year at $2.50/GPU-hr. DLC eliminates this entirely; the MFU recovery is the real DLC ROI, not the electricity bill.
  • FP8 reduces $/token 40% vs. BF16; the full stack (INT4 + FlashAttention v3 + power cap) achieves 78% cost reduction — saving $7.2M/month per 10,000 GPUs at ChatGPT-scale with zero hardware changes.
  • Power capping H100 at 500W vs. 700W improves TFLOPS/W by 13.5%. For cloud providers optimizing margin, lower absolute power may yield higher $/token efficiency despite lower raw throughput.
Principal-Level Insight
  • DLC payback from electricity savings alone takes ~30 years at $0.05/kWh. With MFU recovery included (conservative), payback drops to 2–6 years. Never pitch DLC on power savings — always pitch it on recovered GPU-hours and eliminated throttle events.
  • The $/token formula is the correct optimization target, not raw TFLOPS. It integrates utilization, quantization, power state, and cooling into one metric that aligns hardware, software, and business objectives simultaneously.
  • Owned vs. cloud breakeven (~65–70% utilization) is dominated by GPU amortization, not power. For frontier model companies at 90%+ utilization, owned infrastructure wins by 2–3× on $/GPU-hr. For product companies with bursty demand, cloud spot remains superior regardless of at-scale economics.
