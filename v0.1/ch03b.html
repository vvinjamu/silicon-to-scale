3B.1 How to Read a GPU Roadmap

Before diving into specific chips, establish the right mental model for evaluating any GPU generation. Four lenses determine whether a generation will dominate workloads in production:

The Four Evaluation Lenses ① Compute Density: FLOPs per watt, per dollar, per rack unit. Key metrics: Peak TFLOPS (BF16, FP8, FP4), TDP, TFLOPS/W.
② Memory Architecture: Capacity, speed, scaling. Key: HBM GB, bandwidth TB/s, $/GB.
③ Interconnect: Scale beyond a single GPU. Key: NVLink/Infinity Fabric GB/s, IB integration, topology.
④ Software Stack: Programming model maturity. Key: CUDA/ROCm ecosystem, library coverage, framework support, debuggability.

A GPU that wins on compute but loses on interconnect is useless for training 100B+ parameter models. A GPU with great specs but poor software support costs months of engineering time to deploy productively.

Confidence Labels — Required Reading

Every specification in this chapter carries one of three confidence labels reflecting data reliability as of May 2026:

  • SHIPPED Verified shipping hardware with measured benchmarks from MLPerf or vendor data sheets
  • ANNOUNCED Vendor-disclosed specifications, not yet shipping at publication date
  • ROADMAP From roadmap presentations — treat as directional, subject to change

3B.2 NVIDIA Hopper Generation — H100 and H200

3B.2.1 H100 — The Defining AI GPU SHIPPED

The H100 (2022–2023) is the chip that defined the modern AI infrastructure era. When historians write about the GPU moment in AI, they will cite the H100 as the inflection point where AI compute became a national strategic resource.

H100 SXM5 KEY SPECIFICATIONS: Architecture: Hopper (GH100 die, 80B transistors, TSMC 4nm) Tensor Cores: 4th generation, FP8 native Peak BF16: 989 TFLOPS Peak FP8: 1,979 TFLOPS ← Transformer Engine enabled HBM3: 80 GB, 3.35 TB/s bandwidth NVLink 4.0: 900 GB/s bidirectional per GPU NVSwitch 3.0: Full bisection bandwidth in 8-GPU DGX TDP: 700W (SXM5), 350W (PCIe) Process: TSMC 4nm

Critical Hopper Innovations

① Transformer Engine — Monitors activation magnitudes, computes per-tensor scaling factors, casts to FP8 for GEMM (near-2× throughput), accumulates in FP32. Result: ~1.9× GEMM throughput over pure BF16 with automatic quality preservation.

② FP8 Format Pair (E4M3/E5M2) — E4M3 for weights/activations (precision, limited range ±448); E5M2 for gradients (range, handles magnitude spikes). Together they enable FP8 mixed-precision training without manual per-layer configuration.

③ Thread Block Clusters — Groups of thread blocks sharing distributed SMEM across SMs, enabling super-large GEMM tiles spanning multiple SMs. Required for H100 to achieve near-peak TFLOPS on large matrix ops.

④ NVLink 4.0 / NVSwitch 3.0 — Previous NVLink 3.0: 600 GB/s; H100 NVLink 4.0: 900 GB/s (+50%). NVSwitch 3.0 delivers 3× bandwidth per switch chip, making Tensor Parallelism within 8-GPU DGX dramatically more efficient.

3B.2.2 H200 — Memory Refresh, Not Architecture Refresh SHIPPED

⚠️ Interview Trap "H200 has more TFLOPS than H100." — FALSE.
"H200 is faster at everything." — FALSE.
H200 is the same Hopper GH100 die with upgraded HBM. Identical compute-bound performance. Dramatically better memory-bound performance.
H200 vs H100 — THE CRITICAL DIFFERENCE: H100 → H200 changes: HBM3 → HBM3e (faster interface standard) 80 GB → 141 GB (+76% capacity) 3.35 TB/s → 4.8 TB/s (+43% bandwidth) 700W → 700W (IDENTICAL TDP) TFLOPS → same (IDENTICAL compute units) LLaMA-3 70B in BF16: H100: 140 GB weights → MUST use TP=2 (2 GPUs to fit) H200: 140 GB weights → fits in 141 GB → SINGLE-GPU serving! Result: H200 achieves ~40% more throughput on 70B inference vs H100 despite identical compute — purely from memory capacity/bandwidth.

The H200 shipped in early 2024 as a "soft upgrade" path — same SXM5 form factor, same power envelope, same cooling, allowing customers to swap modules without rack redesign. This is the "server platform continuity" play.

3B.3 NVIDIA Blackwell Generation — B200, GB200, GB200 NVL72

3B.3.1 Blackwell Architecture — The Dual-Die Breakthrough SHIPPED

Blackwell (2024–2025) represents NVIDIA's most dramatic architectural leap since the original GPU. The fundamental design breakthrough is the dual-die MCM (Multi-Chip Module), driven by a hard physical constraint: the EUV reticle limit.

BLACKWELL DUAL-DIE DESIGN: Problem: Modern EUV has a maximum exposure area (~858 mm²) GH100 (H100): 814 mm² — already at the reticle limit Solution: Two dies connected at high bandwidth (NV-HBI) B200 construction: 2× B100 compute dies (each ~450 mm², TSMC 4NP) NV-HBI bandwidth: 10 TB/s (3× H100's HBM bandwidth!) Total transistors: 208 billion (vs 80B on H100 — 2.6×) HBM3e: 192 GB, 8.0 TB/s (2.4× H100) TDP: 1,000W KEY PERFORMANCE: Peak FP8: 4.5 PFLOPS (2.3× H100) Peak NVFP4: 9.0 PFLOPS (new format, B200-native) NVLink 5.0: 1.8 TB/s/GPU (2× H100 NVLink 4.0)

3B.3.2 NVFP4 — The Format That Changes Training Economics

NVFP4 is a 4-bit float: 1 sign bit, 2 exponent bits, 1 mantissa bit. Block scaling lets every 16 weights share one FP8 scale factor, giving better-than-naive-INT4 precision due to floating-point representation.

Why FP4 Matters More Than FP8 Did FP8 vs BF16: 2× throughput gain (H100 era). FP4 vs FP8: another 2× gain. Cumulative: 4× throughput gain from H100 BF16 → GB300 FP4.

More critically: GB300 demonstrated FP4 training in MLPerf v5.1 — the first vendor to achieve FP4 training at scale with accuracy targets met. NVIDIA is the only vendor to submit FP4 training results.

3B.3.3 GB200 NVL72 — The Rack as a Single Computer SHIPPED

GB200 NVL72 ARCHITECTURE: The GB200 "superchip": 1× Grace CPU (ARM Neoverse V2, 72 cores) 2× B200 GPUs NVLink-C2C: CPU↔GPU = 900 GB/s (7× PCIe Gen5) CPU memory: 480 GB LPDDR5X at 960 GB/s (coherent with GPU HBM!) GB200 NVL72 rack — the new unit of compute: 72 B200 GPUs + 36 Grace CPUs NVLink fabric: 130 TB/s total rack bandwidth Compute: 1.4 exaFLOPS FP8 / 2.8 exaFLOPS FP4 HBM memory: 72 × 192 GB = 13.8 TB GPU HBM CPU memory: 36 × 480 GB = 17.3 TB LPDDR5X (coherent) Total: ~31 TB unified (GPU + CPU) Power: ~120 kW per rack Cooling: Direct liquid cooling MANDATORY TENSOR PARALLELISM ADVANTAGE: Standard cluster (H100): TP=8 within node, PP spans nodes via IB GB200 NVL72: TP=72 — ENTIRE RACK as one TP group! Practical result: Models needing 8-way TP (H100) can use 72-way TP at NVLink speeds. Sub-millisecond intra-GPU latency for trillion-parameter inference throughout.

3B.5 AMD CDNA 3 — MI300X

3B.5.1 Architecture: The Memory Moat Strategy SHIPPED

⚠️ Common Misconception — MI300X Architecture WRONG: "MI300X is a 3-chiplet design."
CORRECT: MI300X has 10 dies total — 6 XCDs (compute) + 4 MCDs (memory controllers).
MI300X CORRECT ARCHITECTURE: 6 × XCDs (eXtended Compute Dies): CDNA 3 compute architecture, TSMC 5nm ~50 Compute Units per XCD Total CUs: 304 across all 6 XCDs 4 × MCDs (Memory Controller Dies): HBM controllers, TSMC 6nm 3 HBM3 stacks per MCD = 12 HBM3 stacks total Package: Silicon interposer connecting all 10 dies KEY SPECIFICATIONS: Peak BF16: ~1.3 PFLOPS (1,307 TFLOPS) HBM3: 192 GB, 5.3 TB/s bandwidth Infinity Fabric: ~896 GB/s GPU-GPU (intra-node) TDP: 750W

3B.5.3 MI300X Performance Reality — Honest Assessment

WHERE MI300X WINS: ✓ Memory capacity: 192 GB vs 80 GB (2.4× advantage) ✓ LLM inference (large models): 1×MI300X vs 2×H100 → system-level cost win ✓ Memory bandwidth: 5.3 TB/s vs 3.35 TB/s (+58%) ✓ Long-context (128K+ tokens): full KV cache resident in single GPU ✓ MoE models (Mixtral, DeepSeek): larger expert cache fits in HBM WHERE H100 WINS: ✗ FLOPS/watt: H100 more efficient compute per watt ✗ Tensor Core efficiency: NVIDIA kernel library maturity ✗ Software ecosystem: CUDA, TRT-LLM vs ROCm ✗ Tokens/sec/GPU (small models): H100 wins on latency at batch=1 MLPerf Inference v4.0 — Llama 2 70B, 8-GPU server: MI300X: ~11,700 tokens/sec/GPU H100: ~23,500 tokens/sec/GPU MI300X at ~50% of H100 tok/s per GPU... BUT for 70B: MI300X serves on 4 GPUs vs H100 requires 8 GPUs! System-level (8 GPUs each): H100 (8-GPU, 2× TP groups): ~23,500 tok/s MI300X (8-GPU, 8 single-GPU): ~93,600 tok/s (8 independent models!) AMD's counter: "Compare SYSTEMS not chips." For 70B inference: valid.

3B.6 AMD CDNA 3+ — MI325X

MI325X — THE BRIDGE CHIP (2024): vs MI300X: Same XCD compute dies (identical CDNA 3 compute) HBM3 → HBM3e: 192 GB → 256 GB (+33% capacity) Bandwidth: 5.3 → 6.0 TB/s (+13%) TDP: 750W → ~1,000W KEY TIMING ISSUE: MI325X mass shipments: Q2 2025 B200 launch: Also Q2 2025 Result: Most hyperscalers SKIPPED MI325X ONE NOTABLE WIN: MLPerf Training v5.0 — Llama 2 70B LoRA fine-tuning: MI325X: 21.1 min ← AMD's FIRST-EVER MLPerf Training win! H200: 23.1 min Why AMD won: LoRA keeps base model frozen; larger HBM = more adapter candidates resident simultaneously.
Lesson: Interim Chip Timing Risk Interim chip refreshes only succeed if they ship well ahead of the competitor's next generation. AMD learned this with MI325X — shipping simultaneously with B200 meant most buyers skipped straight to MI355X.

3B.7 AMD CDNA 4 — MI355X

3B.7.1 Specifications and the Competitive Leap SHIPPED

MI355X — CDNA 4 (2025): Architecture: NEW XCD design (not just memory refresh) 2 XCDs (vs 6 smaller ones in MI300X) — fewer cross-die crossings 185B transistors per XCD (high density) FP4/FP6 support: New Matrix Cores (matching B200) HBM3e: 288 GB (vs 256 GB MI325X) Bandwidth: ~8 TB/s (matching B200's 8 TB/s) TDP: ~1,400W Peak FP8: ~5 PFLOPS (vs MI300X ~1.3 PFLOPS — 3.85×) MLPerf Inference v5.0 vs B200 (Llama 2 70B): Offline throughput: MI355X at 97% of B200 ← nearly competitive! Server (interactive): MI355X at 119% of B200 ← AMD WINS latency! AMD winning the interactive scenario is strategically significant: for latency-sensitive apps (chatbots, code completion), MI355X is actually FASTER per-GPU than B200.
Software vs Hardware Contribution to MI355X Gains ~30% of MI355X's MLPerf improvement over MI325X came from software (ROCm 7.1): better FlashAttention ROCm implementation, improved GEMM kernel selection, reduced CPU overhead in ROCm runtime.

Lesson: Hardware matters, but closing the software gap mattered equally. This is AMD's most important signal that ROCm is maturing.
